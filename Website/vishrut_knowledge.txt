After graduating in 2023, Vishrut joined a newly formed school as Assistant Manager, looking after daily operations, conducting events, and solving day-to-day challenges that come with building something from scratch.

Alongside administration, he also taught Mathematics and Physics up to high school level. This was one of the most grounding experiences of his career — having to explain logic clearly to students who had no prior foundation forced him to become a much sharper communicator. He learned how to break down complex ideas into digestible steps, how to read a room, and how to adapt his approach depending on who he was talking to.

In the middle of this role, he identified a growing problem: payment defaults were rising. Rather than treating it as a financial issue, he approached it as a data problem. Using Python and Excel, he mapped fee-payment patterns across the student body and discovered a counterintuitive insight — defaults correlated with weak family-school relationships, not income. Families that felt disconnected from the school's community were the ones most likely to default. He designed a targeted programme of structured parent-teacher interactions and proactive outreach to high-risk families. The result: defaults dropped by approximately 15%, recovering around 300,000 INR — without a single legal notice.

Looking ahead, the skills Vishrut built in this role — clear communication, data-driven decision making, working under ambiguity, and leading without authority — are directly transferable to a career in global strategy and management consulting. He plans to use these to design more resilient systems at a global scale, where the stakes are higher and the complexity even more interesting.
